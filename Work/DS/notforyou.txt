* **1:00 PM - 1:45 PM: BFS vs. DFS (Traversals)**
    * **Logic:**
        * **BFS (Breadth-First):** Go Wide. Uses a **Queue**. (Like throwing a stone in water -> ripples).
        * **DFS (Depth-First):** Go Deep. Uses a **Stack** (internal recursion). (Like a maze solver).
    * **Task:** Learn to draw the traversal order for a simple graph.

* **1:45 PM - 2:30 PM: Dijkstra’s Algorithm (Shortest Path)**

* **2:30 PM - 3:30 PM: Minimum Spanning Tree (Prim’s & Kruskal’s)**
    * **Kruskal’s:** Pick the smallest edge that doesn't form a cycle. (Super easy).
    * **Prim’s:** Grow from a starting node, picking the smallest connected edge.

**Time: 4:00 PM - 6:30 PM**

* **4:00 PM - 4:45 PM: Tree Traversals (Inorder, Preorder, Postorder)**
    * **Task:** Draw a tree. Write the three traversals.

### 🔴 BLOCK 3: HEAPS & QUEUES (The "Easy" Math)
**Time: 7:00 PM - 9:00 PM**
*Why:* Unit 7 (Heaps) and Unit 5 (Queues) are your safety nets.

* **7:00 PM - 7:45 PM: Heap Sort (Unit 7)**
    * **Logic:** A **Max Heap** is a tree where Parent ≥ Children.
    * **Algorithm:** 1. Build Max Heap. 2. Swap Root with Last. 3. Delete Last. 4. Repeat.
    * **Visual:** 
    * **Task:** Perform Heap Sort on `{10, 20, 15, 30, 40}`.

* **7:45 PM - 8:30 PM: Queues (Unit 5 - Concepts)**
    * **Linear Queue:** First In First Out (FIFO). Problem: "Space wastage" (once you delete, you can't reuse space).
    * **Circular Queue:** Connects End to Start. Solves space wastage.
    * **Priority Queue:** Elements come out based on "Priority" (like a VIP line), not arrival time. Implemented using Heaps!
    * **Visual:** 

[Image of Circular Queue diagram]


* **8:30 PM - 9:00 PM: Linked Representation of Queue**
    * **You said you don't know this. Here is the 2-minute lesson:**
        * It is just a Linked List.
        * You maintain two pointers: `FRONT` (Head) and `REAR` (Tail).
        * **Enqueue (Insert):** `Rear->Next = NewNode; Rear = NewNode;`
        * **Dequeue (Delete):** `Temp = Front; Front = Front->Next; delete Temp;`
    * **Task:** Draw a linked list with `FRONT` pointing to node 1 and `REAR` pointing to node 3.
